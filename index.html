Week 1 Reflection

Is google making us stupid?

This was my first lecture in the opening week of the semester. During the session, the tutor raised a thought‑provoking question: does technology make us dumber? To be honest, my initial reaction was yes. With technological advances, especially the rise of AI, people have grown increasingly reliant on these tools, gradually losing opportunities to explore knowledge and think independently. It seemed to me that this would weaken our thinking abilities over time. However, the tutor’s response made me reflect deeply. It is not technology itself that makes us dumber. Technology alone cannot determine whether we become smarter or less capable. It reshapes how we perceive information, sustain attention and store memory — for instance, we no longer memorise large amounts of facts deliberately and outsource memory work to tools. Yet this does not mean our intelligence declines. Outcomes are co‑shaped by an interplay of technology, platform mechanisms, capital, social contexts, and our own choices of how we use these tools. Many technologies are inherently designed to prioritise shallow, instant feedback. Algorithms compete for our attention and can easily get us stuck in superficial thinking. Even so, as users, we retain agency. We can actively decide when to rely on tools, and when to commit to deep reading and independent reasoning. Human beings possess strong individual agency. This lecture has reminded me that throughout my upcoming studies, I should keep exploring actively, think critically and stay committed to learning.

Week 3 Reflection

Web Scraping

In this week’s workshop, we learned about web scraping, which was an amazing experience. As someone with almost no background in computing, it was my first time discovering that we can view the source code behind any website. I immediately wondered whether this would easily lead to website data leaks. Our tutor explained that the source code we can access is only the front-end code for page display. The website’s back-end database and sensitive private user data are not directly exposed. Besides, web scraping comes with legal and ethical limits: we are not allowed to scrape protected, sensitive or restricted data, and platforms also use anti-scraping systems for protection. Next, the teacher demonstrated how to carry out web scraping. It can be quite challenging, but fortunately, we had a web scraping extension that made the task much easier. I tried scraping content from the homepage of BBC Video, and it was really fun.
